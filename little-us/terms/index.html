What you send goes only to the people you choose in that space. Treat their trust carefully: don't share anything from a space outside it without permission.

4. How we enforce these terms

  • Filtering. Little Us automatically checks the words in shared text against a list of prohibited phrases. If a match is found, you'll be asked to change the wording before it's shared.
  • Reports. Anyone who receives content can report it in the app. Reports go to a private review queue.
  • 24-hour action. We review reports within 24 hours. When content breaks these terms, we remove it, and we remove the person who posted it from Little Us.
  • Suspension and removal. We may remove content, take someone out of their spaces, or suspend or close their account, without warning, when we believe they've broken these terms or put someone at risk. Serious cases, such as content that sexualises minors or credible threats, lead to immediate account removal. We report them to the relevant authorities where the law requires it.

5. Reporting and blocking

  • Report a care: tap Report care on a care you received, then pick a reason.
  • Report a note: open the note's ⋯ menu in Keeps and choose Report note.
  • Report a promise: tap Report promise on a promise you were offered or joined.
  • Report a person: go to Space → People and settings, tap ⋯ next to their name, and choose Report.
  • Report a space name: in Space → People and settings, choose Report this room's name.
  • Block someone: go to Space → People and settings, tap ⋯ next to their name, and choose Block person. You'll leave every space you share with them. Neither of you can join a space the other is in while the block is on. Your XP and Stars stay with you. You can unblock someone in You → Account settings → Blocked people.

If you're in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first. Little Us can't respond to emergencies.

6. Cares are kind signals, not proof

Checking off a care means the person tapped Done. Little Us can't see or verify what happens in real life. Don't rely on a check-off as evidence that something was done, especially for health, safety, medication, money or anything else where proof matters.
